# LensS Command Center: demo talk track

The Command Center is now **one story in five chapters**, and the screen follows it. Each chapter opens with a sentence in bold-number text: **that sentence is your line.** Read the opening line, point at the evidence, then use the bridge to move to the next chapter.

**Every number can be opened:** each card has a **View accounts** button that lists the exact accounts behind it (with Export CSV). Use it when someone asks "which ones?", for example on the Today queue: *"here are the 610, with each promise's amount and due date."*

The story bar under the banner (**1 · On track? → 5 · What to do this week**) jumps between chapters and highlights where you are.

Figures are from the 15 September 2026 snapshot. The time is about **8 minutes**.

---

## Opening, about 20 s
**"A collections leader needs five answers every morning: are we on track, how healthy is the book, what's holding us back, where's the money, and what do we do this week? This page answers them in that order. Every number comes from certified data, not AI."**

---

## 1 · Are we on track? (the banner), about 1.5 min
**Say the verdict:** *"We're on track to hit this month's target, but only if customers keep their promises: 65% of the promises already due were broken."*

**Point at:**
- **The bar:** "₹53.4M collected of ₹59.2M, 90%, with 15 days left."
- **The tiles:** "₹5.8M still to collect. Promises due before month-end should bring in about ₹30.3M: 5.2 times what we need. So the outlook is ₹83.7M, 141% of target, and the likelihood is High."
- **The small print:** "And it's honest about how: promises due × the share kept so far. A pipeline view, not a forecast. Data refreshed on…"

**Bridge:** *"So the month is safe on paper, if the promises hold. Before we look at why they don't, how healthy is the book underneath?"*

---

## 2 · How healthy is the book?, about 1.5 min
**Say the opening line:** *"₹1.93B is overdue across 19,035 accounts. 33% of them slipped into a later arrears stage this month, and recovery falls from 4.1% of the balance at 1–30 days to 0.3% at 180+: the earlier we act, the more we recover."*

**Point at:**
- **The cards:** recovery rate 2.77% ("this month's cash against the whole overdue book"); 879 high-risk accounts, 604 of them still likely to pay; 33% worsening vs 6% improving; cost to collect ₹0.016 per ₹1.
- **The risk snapshot:** "A third of accounts are in 1–30 days, where recovery is best; 180+ barely pays."
- *Optional:* **Show 5 more metrics** → "We reach 47% of customers, 46% of those agree to pay, and only about a third keep the promise. Each step leaks."
- *Optional:* **KPI definitions** in the banner: "one definition per metric, everywhere".

**Bridge:** *"A book that's sliding, where speed matters. So what exactly is holding us back?"*

---

## 3 · What is holding us back?, about 1.5 min
**Say the opening line:** *"Broken promises are the biggest threat to the month: 65% of the promises already due were broken. Calling harder won't fix it: 9,393 customers are already contacted 4.5+ times a month."*

**Walk the five ranked issues** (the critical one is the wide card):
1. **Critical: customers are breaking their promises.** 1,234 broke one; 610 more (₹27.2M) due this week are likely to break.
2. **High: Personal Loan is furthest behind**, at 87.6% (₹2.1M short); the largest single gap is Personal Loan at 1–30 days.
3. **High: accounts are sliding into late arrears.** 33% worsened; 999 accounts (₹95M) are about to reach 180+.
4. **Medium: too few customers are reached**: 47% reached, 46% of those agree to pay.
5. **Medium: some customers are over-contacted**: 9,393 customers, a conduct risk.

*Click card 1.* **"Every issue is one click from a deep analysis of why. I'll let this run and come back to it."** *(Return to the Command Center.)*

**Bridge:** *"We know what's wrong. Now, where's the money we can actually get back?"*

---

## 4 · Where is the money?, about 1 min
**Say the opening line:** *"₹6.4M can be recovered now from 604 priority accounts: high-risk customers who are still likely to pay. Credit Card holds the most (₹2.0M)."*

**Point at:**
- **By product:** Credit Card, then Personal Loan, which is also our biggest gap, so that's the focus.
- **The accounts:** "The 8 worth the most, with why they're not paying and the next step for each. Account IDs only; the data has no names or contact details."

**Bridge:** *"So here's what we do about it this week."*

---

## 5 · What should we do this week?, about 1.5 min
**Say the opening line (it's the plan):** *"First save the 610 promises likely to break this week (₹27.2M), then work the 492 high-value accounts likely to pay (₹18.0M), and stop 999 accounts reaching 180+. Every priority customer gets one next step, with vulnerable and disputing customers supported first."*

**Walk the four numbered queues:**
1. **Today: save promises likely to break:** 610 promises, ₹27.2M. Call before the due date. *"This is what protects chapter 1's outlook."*
2. **This week: remind the other promises due:** 588 promises, ₹25.4M, from customers likely to pay. A reminder on their preferred channel is enough.
3. **This week: work high-value accounts likely to pay:** ₹18.0M from 492 accounts (propensity ≥ 60%, balance ≥ ₹100K).
4. **This month: stop accounts reaching 180+:** ₹95.1M across 999 accounts at 150–180 days.

**How to handle each priority customer:**
- **"One rule-based next step per priority customer, and protection comes first:"**
  - disputes go to dispute resolution (72);
  - vulnerable customers go to hardship support (136);
  - customers we haven't reached get a digital journey on their preferred channel (229);
  - the hardest high-balance cases go to a specialist collector (24);
  - everyone else gets prioritised outreach (143).
